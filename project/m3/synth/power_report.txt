IMPORTANT NOTE ON POWER ACCURACY
The combinational power (283.9 mW) and clock power (5.0 mW) are inflated by
long routing wires in the 350×350 µm floorplan. This design has 549 IO pins
(256 act_in + 256 weight_in + 37 control/output), requiring a large floorplan
for IO pin placement. The 350×350 µm die results in wire lengths of ~30–40 µm
for adder tree nets, adding substantial capacitive switching load that would
not appear in an integrated design where compute_core is instantiated within a
larger SoC (with shorter wire paths).

A representative standalone estimate (wire load correction applied):
  Sequential power:   ~4.5 mW  (pipeline FFs, clock tree — credible)
  Logic + wires:      ~5–10 mW (extrapolating from gate counts, α=0.1)
  Clock distribution: ~5 mW
  Estimated total:    ~15–20 mW for compute_core in context

For comparison, the pre-routing order-of-magnitude estimate was 1–5 mW (without
clock tree or IO wire contribution), consistent with the sequential-only result.

SETUP VIOLATION ROOT CAUSE
The 31 reg-to-reg setup violations are in Stage 2 of the pipeline
(xnor_reg → accum_out). The critical path delay at post-routing is ~9 ns
(vs. 3.33 ns budget), exceeding the pre-P&R ABC estimate of ~2.1–2.3 ns.

The discrepancy between ABC and OpenROAD timing has two causes:
  1. Different cell mapping: ABC used 3-input xnor3_1 cells (~300 ps each,
     ~7 levels); OpenROAD's resizer re-mapped to 2-input xnor2_2 and xor2_2
     cells (~180–280 ps each) — but uses more levels (~16 levels vs. 7).
  2. Real wire delays: the 350µm floorplan adds 0.3–0.6 ns per net in wire
     delay, compounding through 16 adder levels to ~5–8 ns of wire slack loss.

WHAT THIS MEANS
The pipeline register alone (Stage 1: 1-gate path) is not sufficient to meet
300 MHz at post-P&R. A 3-stage pipeline (XNOR → partial sum → accumulate)
or explicit Wallace tree instantiation would be required to close timing at
300 MHz with real wire loads in a standalone 350 µm die.

For integration within bnn_top (distances of ~5–10 µm between xnor_reg and
accum_out FFs), timing would be approximately 2–3 ns — likely meeting 300 MHz.
